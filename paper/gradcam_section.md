# Grad-CAM Section (publication-ready draft, 2026-08-13)

> Status: DRAFT — fits into Results/Interpretability. Companion: gradcam_notes_claude.md (raw analysis).

**Interpretability analysis.** To examine the features driving the model's decisions, we computed gradient-weighted class activation maps (Grad-CAM; Selvaraju et al., 2017) from the final convolutional feature map (7×7) for 10 true positives and 10 false negatives from each external dataset. Heatmaps were upsampled to 224×224, rectified, min-max normalized, and blended at 50% opacity over the input. For a subset of false negatives we additionally inspected raw (un-normalized) activation magnitudes to rule out rescaling artifacts.

In true positives from both datasets, attention concentrated within the lung parenchyma in the majority of cases, and no corner markers, laterality text, or image borders were highlighted — ruling out the most blatant shortcut (burned-in annotations). The highlighted region was, however, strikingly stereotyped: near-identical size, shape, and right-upper/mid-lung position across patients. Right-upper-lobe involvement is the most common presentation of post-primary TB, so this pattern is consistent with a coarse, epidemiologically plausible spatial prior; it is not consistent with lesion-specific localization, and the fixed geometry across patients suggests the model weights a lung region rather than detecting discrete pathology. In a minority of cases (2/10 Montgomery), attention shifted to the lower chest.

False negatives exhibited two distinct failure mechanisms. In a subset, the model's strongest activation lay outside the lung fields — most often a horizontal band at the bottom image border — and raw activation magnitudes confirmed this border response was genuine rather than a normalization artifact. In others, lung-region activation was present and anatomically plausible but too weak to cross the decision threshold, indicating a borderline-confidence failure rather than a detection failure. A modest border response (approximately 8–11% of peak activation) was present in all false negatives examined, becoming dominant only when lung evidence was weak.

Taken together, the model localizes to lung parenchyma without burned-in annotation shortcuts, but its fixed right-upper-lung prior and the collapse of attention to non-anatomical regions when that prior is unsatisfied are consistent with coarse spatial-prior learning rather than lesion-specific feature detection. The stereotyped laterality prior is a plausible contributor to the model's failure on the Qatar dataset, where acquisition conditions and image characteristics differ.
